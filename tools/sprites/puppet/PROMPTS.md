# Cut-out voyageur: the parts

Attach `template.png` to each prompt. Save what Gemini makes in this folder
under the name given (PNG if offered). Each prompt asks for the whole
template filled in; the boxes, labels and magenta must stay as they are.

How the pieces are used: each box is one part of the man in that facing,
cut out and pinned to a skeleton at the small cross (the neck, shoulder,
elbow, hip, knee), and the game moves the parts to walk, paddle, shoot.
A garment is the same part drawn dressed; putting on a capote swaps the
torso, arm and hip pieces for the capote's.

## 1. The bare man - `bare.png`

> This template is for a cut-out (paper-doll puppet) game character. Each
> row is one facing - front, side (facing right), back - and starts with a
> reference sprite of the character in that facing. In each labelled box,
> paint **that one body part of the same man, on its own**, in the same
> pixel-art style, colours and outline as the reference, and at the same
> size as it is on the reference shown (the boxes are three times the
> reference's scale). He is **undressed**: bald head with his face and
> black moustache, bare tanned arms, chest and legs, plain grey drawers on
> the hips and thighs, bare feet.
>
> Rules for every part: the part **hangs straight down** from the small
> black cross (the cross is the joint it turns on - the neck for the head,
> which sits above it; the shoulder for the upper arm; the elbow for the
> forearm; the hip for the thigh; the knee for the shin). Fill the box
> top to bottom. **Paint the joint ends rounded and a little overlapping**
> (round shoulder, round elbow, round knee), so the parts still join when
> they bend. Forearm includes the hand; shin includes the foot. Draw one
> arm and one leg per facing (the game mirrors them). Paint nothing outside
> the boxes, leave the reference sprites, labels, boxes and magenta exactly
> as they are.

## 2. His working dress - `dress.png` (give it bare.png)

> Same template, same man, same parts in the same boxes, same size and
> place in each box. Now paint each part **as it looks dressed**: blue and
> white gingham shirt on the torso and down the arms to the wrists (hands
> bare); red wool trousers on the hips and thighs; on the shins red cloth
> leggings to the ankle and brown buckskin moccasins. No sash, no hat - those
> come separately. Change nothing else.

## 3. The capote - `capote.png` (give it bare.png)

> Same template, same parts, same place. Paint the parts as they look in a
> **white wool blanket capote** worn over the shirt: torso and hips in the
> coat (it reaches the knee, so the hips piece is the coat's skirt, a little
> wider), upper arms and forearms in its sleeves to the wrist, two indigo
> stripes near the hem on the hips piece. Head, thighs and shins stay as in
> the bare sheet. Change nothing else.

## 4. Hats, hair and sash - `extras.png`

> Same template, but use the boxes freely for these pieces, one per box,
> each on its own, in the reference's style and size: **row 1 (front)**:
> red wool tuque with a drooping tail and pompom; short black hair (just
> the hair, as it sits on a bald head); arrow sash tied at the waist with
> hanging fringed ends. **Row 2**: the same three pieces seen from the
> side. **Row 3**: the same three from the back. Use the head, torso and
> hips boxes. Leave the other boxes empty.
