# Glossary

Fur-trade vocabulary and the game's own terms, with links to the pages that deal with each. Historical definitions are general knowledge and the design doc's usage; where the game does something specific, it is stated. Terms the game spells differently are given in the game's spelling.

## Fur-trade terms

| Term | Meaning | See |
|---|---|---|
| **Made beaver (MB)** | The Hudson's Bay Company's unit of account: the value of one prime beaver pelt. In game, a beaver pelt is 1.0 MB, a summer (thin) pelt 0.45 MB, a shot-through pelt 0.35 MB, a wolf pelt 0.6 MB, a buffalo robe 2.0 MB. Goods are priced in MB. | [[Made Beaver]], [[Fur Prices]], [[Barter Rates]] |
| **Pelt / pack** | Furs were bundled into packs (about 90 lb) for carrying. The game speaks of "pieces" for a brigade's loads. | [[Furs and Pelts]], [[Brigade]] |
| **Pemmican** | Dried buffalo meat pounded with fat (and sometimes berries) and packed in hide bags; the portable, long-keeping food on which brigades ran. An item in game; made from the plains meat economy. | [[Pemmican]], [[Food and Drink]], [[Buffalo]] |
| **Outfit** | The year's supply of trade goods sent inland to a post. In game the "autumn outfit" is the brigade that leaves York Factory for Oxford House from 18 September. | [[Brigade]], [[Trade Goods]] |
| **Returns** | The year's furs coming out of the interior. In game, "the spring returns" brigade comes down from 8 June. | [[Brigade]] |
| **Brigade** | A convoy of canoes or boats moving together with men, cargo and provisions. | [[Brigade]], [[Transport]] |
| **Portage / carrying place** | A place where canoes and cargo must be carried overland round rapids or between waters. The one place a brigade can be intercepted. | [[Routes and Portages]], [[Brigade]] |
| **Piece (pièce)** | One standard package of cargo carried by one man. | [[Brigade]] |
| **Tumpline** | A leather strap across the forehead or chest used to carry a heavy pack on the back; the "tumpline, pack basket, possibles bag" of the design doc add to a person's carry capacity (planned). | [[Transport]] |
| **Canot du nord / canot du maître** | The north canoe (24 to 28 ft, about 3,000 lb, 4 to 6 men) and the Montreal canoe (up to 40 ft, 7,000 to 9,000 lb, 8 to 14 men). | [[Transport]], [[Canoe]] |
| **York boat** | The Hudson's Bay Company's heavy inland boat, up to 46 ft, up to about 13,000 lb. Planned in game. | [[York Boat]] |
| **Spruce gum** | Resin used to mend a birchbark canoe's hull. In game a brigade carries a few patches. | [[Brigade]] |
| **Factor** | The officer in charge of a Company post (a chief factor over a district). In game the Factor assigns the morning's tasks. | [[Factor]], [[Delegation]] |
| **Postmaster / Clerk** | Ranks below the Factor; a Clerk keeps the books, a Postmaster runs a small post. | [[Clerk]], [[Postmaster]], [[Roles]] |
| **Bourgeois** | The North West Company's name for its partners and for the man in charge of a post or district. | [[NWC Partner]], [[North West Company]] |
| **Wintering partner** | An NWC partner who lived inland and ran a district, as opposed to the Montreal agents who held capital and credit. The Fort William council casts shares for each. | [[NWC Partner]], [[Councils and Accords]] |
| **Agent (Montreal)** | A member of the Montreal firms (McTavish, Frobisher and Co.) that financed the NWC. | [[North West Company]] |
| **Engagé** | A contracted voyageur, hired (often at Montreal) to paddle for a season or several. | [[Voyageur]] |
| **Voyageur** | A canoeman of the fur trade, French-Canadian in origin. | [[Voyageur]] |
| **Mangeur de lard** | "Pork-eater": a Montreal-based canoeman on the Great Lakes leg, as opposed to the winterer (hivernant). | [[Voyageur]] |
| **Hivernant / winterer** | A man who wintered inland. | [[Voyageur]] |
| **Orkneyman** | The Company's labourers largely came from Orkney, whence the "Orkney Scots" voice the game wants for its barks. | [[Labourer]] |
| **Country wife** | A woman, often Cree or Métis, joined to a trader "according to the custom of the country" (à la façon du pays). Marriage tracks and their costs are a design-doc system. | [[Country Wife]], [[Marriage and Country Wives]] |
| **Homeguard** | The Indigenous families who lived near a Company post, hunting for it and trading with it. In game, a homeguard family stands outside every post. | [[Cree Camps]], [[Factions]] |
| **Freeman** | A former servant who stayed in the country as an independent hunter or trader; related to the Métis. | [[Metis]] |
| **Métis** | People of mixed Indigenous and European descent, with a distinct culture, language (Michif) and the buffalo hunt. In game, three Métis camps stand at the Forks, Pembina and the Qu'Appelle. | [[Metis]], [[Red River Colony]] |
| **Capote** | A hooded coat made from a wool blanket; the voyageur's and Métis man's coat. An item in game, with a warmth value. | [[Clothing]] |
| **Point blanket / Stroud blanket** | Heavy wool trade blankets (the point marks the size); "stroud" is a coarse red or blue wool cloth from Stroud, sold by the yard. Both are in game as trade goods. | [[Trade Goods]], [[Items]] |
| **Beads, awl, tobacco** | Staples of trade goods. In game a post that is out of tobacco is "a poor host", because the pipe is smoked before every band's trade. | [[Trading and Barter]] |
| **The pipe** | The opening ceremony of a trade: a pipe filled and passed. In game it is worth twice the movement in the price and twice the warmth of any other gift. | [[Trading and Barter]] |
| **Steel trap** | The metal trap that supplanted the deadfall for beaver. In game, up to six are set at a lodge. | [[Trapping]], [[Beaver]] |
| **Musket (trade gun)** | The flintlock the Company traded to the bands. In game the "trade musket" deals 55 damage and ruins a pelt. | [[Weapons]] |
| **Salt geese** | Geese salted into casks; eight to a cask. The post's winter provision. | [[Goose]], [[Food and Drink]] |
| **The flight** | The goose migration: the autumn flight from 20 August to 15 October is the post's food year. | [[Goose]] |
| **Scurvy** | The disease of a diet without fresh food; the game's main winter killer, countered by fresh meat, fish, berries and spruce beer. | [[Needs and Survival]], [[Health, Wounds and Illness]] |
| **Spruce beer** | A beer brewed from spruce, used against scurvy. | [[Gathering and Gardens]] |
| **Muskeg** | Boggy, peaty subarctic ground; the Hudson Bay lowlands. | [[Terrain and Biomes]], [[Hudson Bay Lowlands]] |
| **Breakup / freeze-up** | The spring ice going out (May and June) and the autumn freezing (September and October). | [[Calendar and Seasons]] |
| **Hudson's Bay Company (HBC)** | The chartered London company holding Rupert's Land; ran posts on the Bay and sent its ship annually. | [[Hudson's Bay Company]] |
| **North West Company (NWC)** | The Montreal-based rival, a partnership of wintering partners and agents; ran canoes to Fort William. | [[North West Company]] |
| **XY Company** | A Montreal rival that merged with the NWC in 1804. | [[XY Company]] |
| **London Committee** | The Company's governing body that sent the year's orders and read the accounts. | [[London Committee]] |
| **Rupert's Land** | The vast territory granted to the HBC. | [[Hudson's Bay Company]] |
| **Red River Colony** | Lord Selkirk's settlement at the Forks (founded 1812). | [[Red River Colony]] |
| **Rendezvous / Fort William council** | The NWC's annual July meeting at Fort William; in game a council of shares and tempers. | [[Fort William]], [[Councils and Accords]] |
| **Hogmanay** | Scottish New Year, the post's biggest feast. Its music is planned. | [[Music]] |
| **Pemmican War** | The conflict of about 1812 to 1816 over the Pemmican Proclamation and Selkirk's colony. | [[Setting and Timeline]], [[Campaigns and Adherence]] |
| **Parole** | A prisoner's promise to carry no arms; in game a captive can give it, and be reported if he does not confess it. | [[Standing]] |
| **Ransom** | A prisoner is worth more loose. | [[Combat]] |

## Game terms

| Term | Meaning | See |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing** | Your reputation with your company; the Factor's report on the ship sets your rank (postmaster at 60, clerk at 35, labourer at 15, otherwise "on notice"). | [[Standing]], [[Ship]] |
| **Ambition** | A declared goal (such as the master's seat). | [[Ambitions]] |
| **Rival** | The ambitious man who wants the same seat as you. | [[Rivalry]] |
| **Favour / Leverage / Grudge / Vendetta** | The ledger of obligations, secrets and wrongs between people. | [[Favours]], [[Gossip, Lies and Leverage]], [[Grudges and Vendettas]] |
| **The Director** | A layer that picks among equally good NPC choices, biasing toward what a player might see. It never decides outcomes. | [[The Director]] |
| **Simulation tiers (Live / Background)** | People near a player are fully simulated; others resolve once a day. | [[Simulation Tiers]] |
| **Adherence** | How hard a campaign holds to history: Gentle, Steady, Firm, Strict. | [[Campaigns and Adherence]] |
| **Reckoning** | The closing of a man's year (with the ship, the canoes, or the ice going out). | [[The Reckoning of 1821]], [[The Year Told]] |
| **Travel mode (G)** | Auto-march to a waypoint at about 25 km per day of game time. | [[Travel and Wayfinding]] |
| **Waypoint** | A marker set by clicking the map. | [[Journal, Map and UI]] |
| **Wildlife site** | A lodge, den, marsh, thicket or herd; the unit of the ecology. | [[Species Sites]], [[Ecology]] |
| **Scarcity** | An over-capacity site whose browse is eaten out; hungry animals range toward the post. | [[Ecology]] |
| **Hunted out** | A fixed site that fell under its sustain floor and will never recover. | [[Ecology]] |
| **The passage** | The months (April, May, September, October) when a herd fills the country. | [[Ecology]], [[Caribou]] |
| **Promotion / demotion** | An abstract count becomes live animals when a player is near, and folds back when far. | [[Ecology]] |
| **Shot-through pelt / holed hide** | A pelt or hide ruined by a musket ball; sells for a fraction. | [[Furs and Pelts]] |
| **Trapline** | The traps you set at a beaver lodge; the catch waits for you. | [[Trapping]] |
| **Poaching** | Robbing another party's trapline. | [[Trapping]], [[Ecology]] |
| **Hotbar** | The nine quick slots, 1 to 9. | [[Inventory and Hotbar]] |
| **Skills** | Hunting, trapping, trade, woodcraft; they improve with use. | [[Skills]] |
| **Sneak** | Moving at half speed so animals notice you at a fraction of the distance and a first blow on an unaware target does 2.5 times damage. | [[Combat]], [[Hunting]] |
| **Chronicle / journal / log** | The world's record, your dated book, and the timestamped notices. | [[Journal, Map and UI]] |
| **Start path** | HBC, NWC, Cree, Assiniboine, or born to any band. | [[Starting Paths]] |
| **Bark** | A short line an NPC says. | [[Dialogue and Conversation]] |
| **Dev argument** | A flag passed after `--` when running the game, such as `--buffalo`. | [[Architecture]] |
