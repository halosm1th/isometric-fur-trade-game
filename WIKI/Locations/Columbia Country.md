## Description

The Pacific slope has the most posts of any region (20), but most are after the window: Fort Vancouver (1825), Colvile (1825), Langley (1827), Nisqually (1833), Victoria (1843), Connolly (1826), Babine (1822), Chilcotin (1829) and Dease Lake (1838) never spawn in a 1811 to 1821 game. The in-window posts are New Caledonia's NWC chain (Fort McLeod 1805, Fort St. James and Fort Fraser 1806, Fort George (BC) 1807), Kootenay House (1807, closed 1812), Spokane House (1810), Flathead House (1809), Fort Kamloops (1812), Fort Nez Perces (1818) and Fort Alexandria (1821). Fort Okanogan (PFC 1811) is a map point only.

The campaign has the `astoria-sold` beat (16 October 1813: Astor's Fort Astoria sold to the NWC as Fort George), but the post itself is not on the map.

## Peoples and camps

Camps in this region: Kootenay, Salish (Flathead), Coast Salish, Carrier (Dakelh), Tsilhqot'in, Sekani, Secwepemc, Syilx and Nlaka'pamux bands, and the family camps at each post. See [[Country Camps]] for the full list.

## Wildlife

Beaver country (about 55 lodge sites), with hare, caribou and three buffalo plains; wolf and goose sites along the coast.

## Tips

- New Caledonia's posts are among the farthest from York: the brigade route from Fort Vancouver to Fort St. James is 1,235 km on its own.
