## Description

The Hudson Bay lowlands are the game's first region and its only fully built one. A flat country of muskeg, tamarack and spruce running down to a shore with an ice-bound bay, they hold the Company's oldest factories: **Moose Factory** (1673), **Albany Fort** (1679), **Fort Severn** (1689), **York Factory** (1684) and **Churchill** (1717). Ships from London reach York only between the 20th of August and the 15th of September, and the sea is open "for about two months a year".

The region is the heart of the **Hayes route**: from York up the Hayes to Oxford House (1798), then on toward Norway House on Lake Winnipeg and the Saskatchewan brigade. The James Bay side (Moose, Albany, Henley House, Gloucester House, Martin Falls, Osnaburgh House, Brunswick House) is the **Southern Department**, a separate HBC division headquartered at Moose Factory rather than York (design doc §6.2): its own region "because the point is to replicate the structure of the HBC and NWC as closely as possible across about 1801-1821". Great Whale River (1820) and Fort George (Chisasibi, 1837) sit on the eastern shore.

The Nor'Westers have one rival house on the Bay in the game, up-country from York ([[Nor'Westers' House]]); inland posts of the NWC on this side are few.

## Peoples and camps

Camps in this region: the Swampy Cree, Oji-Cree and the family camps at Moose Factory, Albany Fort, Fort Severn, Churchill, Henley House, Gloucester House, Martin Falls, Osnaburgh House, Brunswick House, Island Lake House, Great Whale River and Fort George (Chisasibi); plus the Cree and Assiniboine camps at York (see [[Cree Camps]], [[Assiniboine Camps]]). See [[Country Camps]] for the full list.

## Wildlife

The region carries the York Factory sites (goose marsh 180/300, beaver lodges at upper/bend/lower/Knee Lake/Oxford, the caribou herd 90/140 on its seasonal route, the wolf den 6/10) and, about each Bay post, goose marshes, ptarmigan flats, hare flats, caribou herds and wolf dens. By this wiki's grouping the region holds about 24 caribou, 25 goose, 38 beaver, 23 wolf, 36 ptarmigan and 15 hare sites (see [[Species Sites]]). The goose is the Bay's staple: the autumn flight (20 August to 15 October) is the one event a Bay post cannot afford to miss.

## Tips

- The ship, not the road, is the Bay's lifeline: be at York for 20 August to 15 September.
- Winter is the road: the frozen river and bay are the highway (pace 1.25, slippery).
- Snow drags (0.7) unless you wear snowshoes; muskeg in summer is heavy going (0.75).
- Berries (saskatoon, cranberry, rosehip) and spruce beer answer the scurvy; see [[Gathering and Gardens]].
