## Description

Athabasca is the NWC's "emporium of the north" and the farthest the brigades go in the main game window. The English River posts (Ile-a-la-Crosse 1779, Lac la Ronge 1782, Green Lake) sit on the road, and beyond **Methye Portage** (a 20-km divide between the Churchill and Mackenzie watersheds) lie Fort Chipewyan, the Peace River post of Dunvegan (1805), Lesser Slave Lake House (1802), Fort Assiniboine (1824, after the window), Jasper House (1813, guarding the Athabasca Pass route) and Fort Nelson (BC, 1800).

HBC's rival post at Fort Chipewyan, Fort Wedderburn (1815), centre of the 1815 to 1821 violence, is not on the map.

## Peoples and camps

Camps in this region: the Chipewyan band (cell 469, 416), the Dene (Beaver) band, and the family camps beside Fort Chipewyan, Lesser Slave Lake House, Dunvegan, Fort Nelson and Fort Assiniboine. See [[Country Camps]] for the full list.

## Wildlife

Caribou herds in "Fort Chipewyan country", "Dene (Beaver) country" and "Fort Nelson (BC) country" are the largest in the game (about 1,800 caribou across the region's sites); beaver is plentiful (about 46 lodges). By this wiki's grouping the region has 46 beaver, 18 caribou, 10 ptarmigan, 12 wolf and 9 hare sites.

## Tips

- The Methye carry is the crux: a heavy canoe load and a thin crew is the quickest way to lose a brigade.
- The Dene bands are the hunters; family camps stand beside Fort Chipewyan, Lesser Slave Lake House, Dunvegan and Fort Nelson.
