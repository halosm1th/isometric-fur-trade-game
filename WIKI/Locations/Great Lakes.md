## Description

The Great Lakes corridor is the NWC's highway: the Montréal canoe route runs from the map's east edge (past La Cloche and Sault Ste. Marie) up the north shore of Lake Superior to [[Fort William]], then west by Rainy Lake and Lake of the Woods to the Winnipeg River and Fort Bas de la Riviere. The 13.5-km Grand Portage and the later Kaministiquia route both lie here. The south-east corner has the lakes' entrepot Michilimackinac (1780) and Sault Ste. Marie (1783).

Rat Portage (HBC 1836) is after the window; Lac Seul House (HBC) is founded in 1815.

## Peoples and camps

Camps in this region: the Plains Ojibwa and Ojibwa (Anishinaabe) bands, the Odawa, Menominee and Ho-Chunk bands, and family camps at every post. See [[Country Camps]] for the full list.

## Wildlife

Beaver country (about 50 lodge sites), caribou, hares and wolves, with Ojibwe families beside every post.

## Tips

- The Montréal route is the long way home for NWC furs; the July rendezvous gathers the partners at Fort William.
- Winter freezes the lakes: they become the road (but slippery).
