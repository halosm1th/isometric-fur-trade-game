# All Trading Posts

**Status:** Partial. The data is complete (105 entries in `Data/World/world.json`), but only a few posts have custom buildings; most are a generic palisade and house with a three-person cast.

![[map-posts-hbc-nwc.png|700]]

`world.json` lists **105 places**: 100 posts, the rival "Nor'Westers' house (York)" and four camps of the York country. Generated by `tools/world/build_world.py` from the *Pemmican Empire* atlas data plus `tools/world/extras.json`. The campaign decides which posts stand: by default a post stands when its founding year is on or before the start year (1811) and it has not closed; in the historical campaign (`posts_by_founding_year`) each later post is founded on **1 January of its year** and gains a palisade, house, cast and books. See [[Campaigns and Adherence]].

> [!warning] Posts that never spawn
> `world-content-needed.md` §4.4 says posts dated after 1821 should be left in but should never spawn: Fort Pitt 1829, Fort Pelly 1824, Fort Ellice 1831, Fort Qu'Appelle 1864, Upper/Lower Fort Garry 1822/1830, Rat Portage 1836, Touchwood Hills 1852, Fort Vancouver 1825, Fort Nisqually 1833, Fort Colvile 1825, Fort Langley 1827, Fort Victoria 1843, Fort Connolly 1826, Fort Babine 1822, Dease Lake 1838, Fort Chilcotin 1829, Fort Assiniboine 1824, Fort George (Chisasibi) 1837, and the American Missouri posts (Piegan, Benton, Union, Clark, McKenzie, Berthold). The game founds a post only when the calendar reaches its year, so the 1822+ posts stay out of a 1811-1821 game. Great Whale River (1820) is the one the document lists that actually falls inside the window. The Pacific coast anchor of the period, Fort Astoria/George (1811), is *not* on the map; neither are Fort Wedderburn, Fort Daer or Jack River House (see the [[Roadmap]]).

> [!info] How a post is built
> A post in the `layout.posts` section (86 of them) is painted as a **generic** 12 x 9 palisade with a gate at offset 5 and one 7 x 5 house ("hearth, counter, bunks, the bales") at (2,2), a woodpile and a crate, a name sign outside the gate, and gets a master plus two men as cast. Six posts have a **custom plan** in `post_plans.json` (Fort William, Moose Factory, Edmonton House, Cumberland House, Fort Chipewyan, Fort Gibraltar). York Factory, Oxford House and the Nor'Westers' house are **hand-built** in `WorldLayout`. The 12 remaining posts (French and American, plus Fort Okanogan) are **map points only**: no palisade, house, cast or books. A post given up before the clock (`post_closures`) never stands, but one that closes later (Kootenay House, 1812) is not removed once standing.

## Notes on the data

- **Cells.** Each place has a map `cell` (its real lon/lat run through the projection) and, for posts with a palisade, a fitted `top` corner nearby on dry ground: the importer found "the nearest dry spot" to the real position (see [[World Map]]). The two can differ by tens of cells.
- **Double entries.** Fort la Reine and Portage la Prairie share a cell (the NWC post stands on the French site). Edmonton House / Fort Augustus, Fort George / Buckingham House and Brandon House / Fort Souris are the paired NWC and HBC posts the atlas shows side by side.
- **Name clashes.** Fort George appears three times: the 1792 North Saskatchewan NWC post (closed 1800), Fort George (BC) on the Fraser (NWC, 1807) and Fort George (Chisasibi) on James Bay (HBC, 1837). Fort Alexandria likewise (Assiniboine 1795; Fraser 1821). The Fort Qu'Appelle in the data is the **1864** HBC post, not the NWC pemmican post of the 1810s.
- **Closing year.** `Closed` comes from the layout posts' `closed` field (`extras.json` `post_closures`, "from memory and want checking" for most of them).
- **Cast.** Only posts with a layout post have `NpcSpawn` markers in the scene, and only those standing get people: a master and two men (generic) up to 10 people at Moose Factory and Fort William.
