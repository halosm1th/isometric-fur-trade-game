- Rather than seeing history as collectives of race/class/gender focus on viewing individuals by considering their **Ancestry, Associations, and Status**  as well as considering location, age, and sex. 
	- The individual, the agent through whom each of these institutions are filtered and defined. The perception of these institutions both individual and collective. The individual holds a part but not the whole of how they are defined. 
	- Cultural institutions
		- These define how an individual defines and relates to their in group, as well as to the world at large. These are largely the places which grant honour to an indiviudal. 
		- Ancestry - one's lineage, whos one's parents are, how one grew up, the history and stories assocatied with that. Not only material but metaphysical as well. 
			- For example, the bastard son of a noble and the heir of a noble are both defined in part by their shared ancestry. Similarly, the nobility tends to bond together based on a shared history and intermarriage, same with the lower class, this creates ancestreal bonds to those group identities which can pass through generations. 
			- It is through ancestry which race can be explored. As ultimately the one drop rule is a scientific and racist veneer over the worst descriminations associated with prejudices of birth via ancestry. Whether racial, religious, class, familial or otherwise. 
		- Associations - which groups does the individual associate with. Who are the people whom an individual knows, and how are those friendships related to the groups the people are a part of. This is not only the the question of who is someone's employer, but also what is their religion - to whom do they confess their sins, what is their nationality, and where does someone go to find their friends.
			- These institutions are an essential part of class formation, protection, and reproduction. They are also an essential part of where an individual earns honour and comes to define themselves. How these institutions relate to the law and government, as well as an individual, and their family's own relationship to such groups, all impact how one earns honour and reputation. If one construes themselves to be a good person by having a positive association with the common 'community' than it follows that when they committee a crime, even if driven by necessity, they perseves themselves to have been dishonoured. Alterantively if one does feels closer to the common outgroup than the in-group, anti-social actions may be rewarded with praise and honour. Through this, as class identities become more defines class tensions may rise, though other tensions may be generated instead, such as racial, religious, or other more petty forms of competitive hierarchical interaction, driving innovation.  
		- Status - status refers to one's position within various honour granting groups or associations, filtered through the prism of 'common opinion' of the dominant cultural group. The legitmacy of that dominant group allows them to grant legitmacy to other groups aligned with the dominant group, and attempt to deny it to those non-dominant groups whom it is political convinenty to deny it to. As power shifts between individuals, the assocaitions they form, and the institutions those associations construct, and individuals join, leave, and start new while drawing on the old, the groups which grant status change. So long as the political, social, economic, and other structures are able to grant adapt to let new in groups rise while out-dated ones fall, without letting in so many new groups that those old and venerated groups which garnerd the legimacy in the first place fall to factionism and infighting, the institutions remain legimtate.  
			- Status thus transcends 'class', rather serving as a cross class or assocation set of signals for various interst groups to connect and follow around. The best political leaders need connections across all levels and the ability to hear the needs from all quarters of society, if they are isolated to only one level they will not be effective in their roles.  
			- Status serves to flag people who can communiate the needs of a section of society. They are granted status because they are able to see their follows needs fulfilled. In fulfilling needs, the fulfilled person honours the fulfiller, thereby increasing the fulfillers status. Over time, as these people become friends and honour is transmitted between them, likeminded individuals form assocations. These assocations then confer status by their members honouring one another, recognizing each others acomplishments. 
			- Those who gain status for being able to fulfill political needs connect with other people who have political status, allowing quid pro quos to be arranged. The same happens in other areas of specialization.
